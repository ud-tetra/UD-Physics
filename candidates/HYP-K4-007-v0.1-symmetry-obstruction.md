## Question
Does native K4 finite-state structure uniquely force a seven-state restriction without empirical targets?

## Admitted toy carrier and assumption
Let S = S4 × Theta, |S4| = 24, |Theta| = 8, hence |S| = 192.
**Assume** the left-regular action g·(h,t)=(gh,t), leaving the Theta coordinate unchanged. This is a test model, NOT a sourced assertion that it is UD's unique physical action.

## Exact theorem
Each orbit under this action is S4 × {t}, hence has cardinality 24. Every invariant subset Q is a disjoint union of orbits, so |Q|=24k for an integer k in [0,8]. Therefore an invariant restricted subset with |Q|=7 cannot exist under this assumed action.

## Independent elementary check
If Q contains (h,t), invariance requires (gh,t) for all g in S4, hence all 24 members of that fiber. Conversely, unions of complete fibers are invariant. No empirical constants enter.

## Important non-conclusions
- Does not rule out seven distinguished Theta patterns (e.g., nonzero 3-bit words): these are seven *fiber labels*, not seven individual states of S.
- Selecting seven of eight Theta labels yields 7×24=168 states, not seven.
- Does not rule out other group actions, equivariant projections, dynamic constraints, gauge quotients, stabilizers, orbit weighting, non-invariant sectors, or seven restricted modes rather than seven states.
- Does not establish that the actual UD 192-shell uses the assumed regular action or that the proposed seven-state quarantine is false.
- Exact arithmetic 192−7=185 does not supply a unique physical selection rule.
