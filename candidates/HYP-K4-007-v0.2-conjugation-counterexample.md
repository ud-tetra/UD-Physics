## Question
Does S4 symmetry by itself prohibit or uniquely select seven restricted states in a 192-element S4 × Theta carrier?

## Two distinct actions
Let S=S4×Theta with |Theta|=8 and fix a fiber label t0.
A. Left-regular: g·(h,t)=(gh,t). Orbits have 24 elements; invariant sets have sizes divisible by 24. Thus 7 is impossible (v0.1 conditional no-go).
B. Conjugation: g·(h,t)=(ghg^{-1},t). This is a valid S4 action on the same 192-element set, but is NOT established as the UD physical action.

## Explicit exact seven-element invariant set under B
Let T be the set of six transpositions in S4, and e the identity. Define Q=({e} ∪ T)×{t0}.
Then |Q|=1+6=7. Conjugation fixes e and sends transpositions to transpositions, so Q is invariant under all g∈S4.

## Proof and interpretation
A group's abstract identity S4 does not uniquely specify its action on the 192-element carrier. Consequently, symmetry invariance alone cannot decide the seven-state question without specifying the actual action. The v0.1 obstruction remains true for A; B supplies a counterexample to any unrestricted claim that 'S4 invariance forbids seven'.

This example does NOT show that Q is the native UD quarantine, that the choice of t0 is canonical, or that the seven states are uniquely selected. Indeed eight choices of t0 yield eight such Q subsets absent a distinguished fiber. Nor does it establish a physical correspondence.
