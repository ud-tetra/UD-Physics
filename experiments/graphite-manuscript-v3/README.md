The paper retains the conventional $\cos\theta=13/14$ paired-angle account and the passive three-wave kinetic control. It now distinguishes the native signed edge-amplitude response from an even stationary variance source: physical time reversal excludes a linear equilibrium cross-Hessian under the stated identification, and an isotropic quadratic canonical ensemble gives uniform edge variance and zero mean source. A posited C3 local stiffness defect yields $\delta=-\Theta\alpha/(1+\alpha)$, but selects neither index seven nor friction amplitude. This is a changed constitutive assumption, not a graphite material derivation.

The exact counterexample uses $K_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,4)$ and $K_2=\operatorname{diag}(4,1)$ with $C=(1,1)$ and $D=I$. Both have the same eigenvalue set, per-mode squared coupling, scalar softening $5/4$ and low-frequency drag $17/16$, while the variance of registered coordinate one differs by a factor of four. The archived 16 Å stationary KC-plus-plate mode-summary extremes imply only a Rayleigh bound with a width ratio of about 425.14; the archived full mixed Hessian comes from another reconstruction. Neither identifies a site/edge covariance at the stationary state. This is a data identifiability result, not a claim of graphite anisotropy.

**Next discriminant:** preserve a matched-state full registered $K$ and $C$, equilibrium covariance/site mapping, measured complex loss $D(\omega,P)$, pressure variations and contact transfer; freeze an ordinary baseline and then forecast the signed force/load residual at paired angles and controls before viewing held-out traces. The accessible [Chen et al. PRL abstract](https://doi.org/10.1103/ly48-b4l2) reports *peaks* with weak load dependence, not exactly zero friction.
